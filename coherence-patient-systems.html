Scale vs. Coherence

A Fortune 5 health services enterprise was growing fast. Through rapid acquisition, it had amassed independent physician groups, clinics, and capabilities across the U.S. While the strategic intent was unity, the operational reality was fragmentation.

The organization inherited a patchwork of portals, call center tools, and EHRs. They had an enterprise CX measurement framework, but it was unevenly applied. Some regions had robust analytics; others flew blind. The leadership needed more than "another tool"—they needed a connective tissue that could link 150,000 clinicians to a single, coherent view of the patient experience.

Our Role

We didn't just map the journey; we engineered the governance to support it. Our team partnered with the enterprise to:

  • Forensic Mapping

    Creating the first true inventory of digital and MarTech capabilities across the organization.

  • The Blueprint as Governance

    Designing a "Future State" service map that acted as the architectural requirement for all new integrations.

  • Instrumentation

    Moving from abstract NPS scores to deeply integrated signals that tied experience data to operational workflows.

1. Forensic Mapping: Replacing Assumptions with Facts

The first job was to replace assumptions with facts. We ran a forensic MarTech and Digital Experience assessment across the regional entities, cataloging exactly how each Care Delivery Organization (CDO) acquired, served, and retained patients.

We positioned each region on a digital maturity curve. This revealed the "Operational Gravity" holding the organization back:

Key Challenges Identified:

  • • Identity Fragmentation: Patient records were fractured between clinical and digital systems.
  • • Platform Sprawl: Multiple tools doing the same job (schedulers, portals) created inconsistent experiences.
  • • Measurement Gaps: The CX framework existed in theory, but in practice, regions defined "engagement" differently, making enterprise-level governance impossible.

This assessment gave leadership a single, comparative picture of the ecosystem—shifting the conversation from "what we think is happening" to "what is actually happening."

2. The Blueprint as Governance

With the ecosystem mapped, we shifted to the core design artifact. We needed a service blueprint that could withstand the weight of regulatory compliance and legacy debt.

To keep the work human, we anchored everything around William, a 65-year-old Medicare patient managing diabetes. His journey—from finding a provider to managing his chronic condition—became the spine of the blueprint.

But the innovation wasn't the map itself; it was the annotation layer:

  • The Front Stage

    We defined exactly what William sees (SMS, Portal, Clinic).

  • The Back Stage

    We mapped the specific plumbing required to support him (Salesforce Health Cloud for orchestration, Adobe AEM for content, Epic for clinical data).

  • The "No-Go" Check

    The blueprint became a negotiating instrument. When a region wanted to buy a non-compliant tool, leadership could point to the blueprint and ask, "Where does this fit in William's journey?" If it broke the flow, it wasn't approved.

3. Instrumentation: Moving from "Scores" to Signals

The goal was not to invent a new measurement model, but to operationalize the existing one. We moved beyond "Survey Architecture" to "Operational Intelligence."

We asked: When William transitions from a telehealth visit to a lab test, does the measurement framework capture that handoff? If not, the metric is blind.

We focused on three shifts:

  • Align Metrics to Moments

    We mapped existing CX metrics (NPS, CSAT) to specific stages in William's journey.

  • Connect Behavior to Operations

    We rounded out the framework with operational signals—First Contact Resolution, Digital Adoption Rates, and Care Plan Adherence. This allowed us to correlate "high portal usage" with "lower call center volume," proving the ROI of the design.

  • Centralize the Signal

    We defined a central CX dashboard that pulled standardized metrics from CRM, portals, and call centers, allowing leaders to see regional performance side-by-side for the first time.

The Impact: From Reactive Fixes to Proactive Improvement

While the full rollout is a multi-year effort, the combination of forensic assessment, blueprinting, and aligned measurement has fundamentally changed how the enterprise operates.

By seeing the full system and aligning CX measurement to that map, the organization moved from plugging leaks one by one to systematically closing structural gaps.

  • A "North Star" for Decisions

    The service blueprint is now the reference point for patient experience work. New platform decisions are checked against it to ensure they reduce friction for patients like William.

  • Decisions with Intent

    The gap analysis directly fed the technology roadmap, driving commitments to a Universal Patient Identity layer and a single enterprise patient portal.

  • Proof in the Pilot

    In one pilot region, moving a clinic group to the unified portal increased online appointment booking from 20% to 50%. Because the CX measurement framework was wired into the new flow, leadership could see the impact on satisfaction and repeat engagement immediately.
